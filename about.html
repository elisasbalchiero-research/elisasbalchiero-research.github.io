Short bio

Elisa Sbalchiero

I am a PhD researcher in Quantitative Sociology at the Graduate School in Social and Political Sciences, University of Milan.

My methodological interests centre on longitudinal and multilevel methods for social research. My substantive interests include education, social inequality, and education policy reforms, as well as the development of civic competencies, students’ political engagement and related inequalities, and school-to-work transitions.

In my own words

Background, research, and beyond

A brief account of how education, places, and interests have shaped my academic trajectory.

01

Educational background

I earned a Bachelor’s degree in Sociological Sciences at the University of Padua and a Master’s degree in Sociology and Social Research at the University of Trento. My bachelor’s thesis examined the educational trajectories of second-generation migrant students and the role of families and teachers. My master’s thesis focused on upper-secondary school placement and related achievement disparities between students with and without a migrant background, considering the contribution of family and school factors. Across both projects, I was drawn to education’s promise of equalisation, pathways of social mobility, and the persistence of social inequalities in educational outcomes.

02

Research interests

My research investigates why social inequalities persist in contemporary societies, with particular attention to education and educational trajectories. I examine how interconnected spheres of influence—including families and parental resources, neighbourhoods, schools, teachers, peer contexts, and features of education systems—combine to shape students’ opportunities and outcomes.

03

Academic path

Trento strongly shaped my academic path and was where I decided to pursue a PhD. Yet my interest in, and passion for, social research began in Padua and has never left me. A curricular internship at Ghent University’s Department of Sociology during my master’s programme helped consolidate my interest in multicultural education, ethnic discrimination in education, inequalities by students’ social and migration backgrounds, and the role of teachers and school environments.

04

Beyond research

I am reflective by nature and a keen observer. Beyond research, I care deeply about grassroots politics, radio, and drawing—especially in graphite, although I have recently begun working with colour. I collaborate with Vag61, Bologna’s longstanding social centre, and the Lorusso-Giuliani Documentation Centre for Social Movements to catalogue and archive press materials relating to the period before, during, and after the 2001 G8 summit in Genoa.
